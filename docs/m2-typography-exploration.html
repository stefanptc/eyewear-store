M2 / 02 — Typography

Three pairings, one product. Pick by gut.

Direction locked: A / A / D Serif-led · Mono+accent · Type-led
Brief. Same product, same copy, same layout — only the typeface pair changes. Read each column slowly. Notice how the page feels, not just how it looks. The serif carries 60% of the brand; if it's wrong, nothing else saves it. Romanian diacritics (ă â î ș ț) are stressed throughout — watch how each serif handles them. The accent colour (deep oxblood) is a placeholder; we lock the real one with the typeface, not before.

Fraunces — display
  • Variable font, optical sizing axis (opsz)
  • "SOFT" axis can warm letterforms without losing structure
  • Strong italics with distinct character
  • Risk: can read slightly "cute" if SOFT is too high
Inter Tight — body
  • Condensed cousin of Inter — same DNA, more editorial bearing
  • Excellent Romanian support, broad weight range
  • Risk: still recognisably "Inter family," very common online

Source Serif 4 — display
  • Variable font with optical sizing
  • Adobe-designed, refined for editorial use
  • Less personality than Fraunces — more "transparent"
  • Risk: can read as quiet to the point of forgettable at small scales
IBM Plex Sans — body
  • IBM's corporate workhorse — broad weights, excellent diacritics
  • Slight technical/architectural character in the letterforms
  • Risk: more associated with "tech brands" than eyewear

EB Garamond — display
  • Garamond revival by Georg Duffner, excellent extended-language support
  • Old-style, classical, most "archival" of the three
  • Runs small — needs +15% size to balance the others
  • Risk: closest to "book" not "shop" — can feel academic
Public Sans — body
  • USWDS government typeface — neutral, broad language support
  • Most "transparent" of the three sans — least character
  • Risk: too neutral; can feel anonymous next to the classical serif

What to look at — and what to ignore

Look at: how the product name "Ochelari Mărășești" feels in italic. How the diacritics sit on top of the letters (ă, â, î) — do they look balanced, or stuck on like afterthoughts? Whether the body copy reads quietly next to the display (you want body to recede, display to lead).

Ignore: the oxblood accent, the layout, the button styles, the photography (there is none). All of those are still TBD. The only variable here is type.

My bias, for transparency: 01 is the safe bet, 03 is the boldest bet, 02 is the diplomatic middle. Cubitts itself uses something closer to 03 than 01 — but Cubitts has decades of brand equity to carry an "academic" feel. We don't. 01 with a careful SOFT axis pull might be where this lands.
